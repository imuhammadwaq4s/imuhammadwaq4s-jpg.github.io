Research

Powering sensors in the field

My doctoral work explores whether ambient RF signals and interference can support energy-constrained devices outdoors, with potential applications in agriculture and environmental monitoring.

University of Maine · 2026–present

Low-power wireless sensing

I’m investigating ambient RF energy harvesting and power management for distributed sensors in places with limited infrastructure. Potential applications include monitoring plant health and soil conditions.

Westlake University · 2026

Non-invasive health monitoring

During a summer research internship, I studied optical and physical sensing techniques for a smart-toilet platform, including ways to measure color, flow, temperature, and morphology.

UESTC · 2025–2026

Hybrid RF and triboelectric energy harvester

My master’s project combined a 2.45 GHz RF harvester with a motion-driven triboelectric nanogenerator for wearable health sensors. The integrated prototype delivered 1.446 mW in bench testing.

GCUF · 2022–2023

Smartflower solar tracker

I developed an Arduino-controlled prototype that follows the sun and retracts in rain. In project testing, it captured about 40% more energy than the fixed-panel comparison setup.
